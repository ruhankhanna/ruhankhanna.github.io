athletIQ

Founder. Rebuilt from scratch in November 2025; 314 commits since. Flask, SQLite, Selenium, SciPy, Railway.

College track recruiting still runs on spreadsheets and a single personal best. athletIQ is what a coaching staff uses instead: it finds recruits across every state, puts every event on one scale, forecasts where an athlete is headed, and tracks them from first contact to the roster.

  1. Scraper service. Fetching runs as its own process that the app calls over an authenticated API, so one slow page never stalls a coach's session.
  2. Every state, every season. Ranking pages are keyed by season IDs; the app discovers them for all 50 states and DC and caches them to disk.
  3. Background jobs. A national search can touch thousands of athletes, so it runs off the request thread while the page polls for progress.
  4. Tools on top. Plain-English search that fills in the filters, a recruit pipeline from first contact to commitment, and roster tracking with eligibility and redshirts.

Search a whole country against your standards

A coach sets a target mark for each event, a tolerance band, states and a graduating class. A background job pages through every matching state ranking, scores each athlete's distance from the standard (within 1% scores 100, falling off past 5%), merges each athlete's events across states by profile, and rewards athletes who fit in more than one event. Results export straight to a mail-merge CSV with school contacts.

One scale for every event

An 800m runner and a 5000m runner can't be compared on time. athletIQ converts every race to VDOT, Jack Daniels' oxygen-cost model, and uses it to compare recruits across events, project high school marks to college, and check how a recruit fits the current roster.

VO₂ = −4.60 + 0.182258v + 0.000104v², divided by the fraction of max sustainable for that duration. Going back from VDOT to a time is a bisection search.

Forecasting from training, not just results

The forecaster fits a Banister fitness-fatigue model to each athlete: every training load adds fitness that fades slowly and fatigue that fades fast, and performance is the difference. athletIQ fits the gains and time constants to that athlete's own races with regularized least squares, then samples 4,000 parameter sets from the fit's covariance to draw an honest range.

Fitness-fatigue modelDrag the weekly mileage bars. The band is 300 sampled athletes.

Here τ-fitness and τ-fatigue sit at the product's priors (45 and 11 days) and the band varies them with a ±15% responder spread; the product fits them per athlete.

Benchmark before you ship

To decide how athletIQ should flag injury risk, I tested three approaches on a public dataset of 42,798 athlete-weeks from 74 runners, splitting folds by athlete so no runner appeared in both training and testing. Plain logistic regression beat XGBoost and the acute-to-chronic workload ratio that coaches commonly use.

AUC is how well a model ranks injured weeks above healthy ones; 0.5 is chance. The honest result is modest, which is the point of measuring.

Meridian

Lead researcher, UVA School of Data Science, with Prof. Adam Tashman. 2025 to now. LightGBM, sentence-transformers, FastAPI.

Venture capital is a market for founders, and it has frictions: who you are and where you're from shift who funds you. Meridian measures those frictions and turns the model around into a tool that matches underserved founders with investors likely to fund them.

It is a learning-to-rank problem, not a classifier. For each startup, a LightGBM LambdaRank model orders 48,046 investors, trained on about 907,000 startup-investor pairs. Each pair gets 16 features, including embedding similarity between the startup's description and the investor's portfolio (MiniLM sentence embeddings), stage and check-size fit, geography, and how concentrated the investor's past deals are. The split is by startup, so no company appears in both training and test, and the candidate pool only includes investors active in that deal's year.

We deployed it with founder organizations including 434, BEA, StartOut, Astia and BGV, serving investor matches to more than 20,000 underserved founders. The paper is in review; I presented it at the 2026 UVA Undergraduate Research Symposium and ICMBF 2026.

Pan-Out

Solo build, 2026. Python, scikit-learn, a standard-library HTTP server, plain JavaScript.

Ratings ignore age: a 13-year-old and a 17-year-old with the same rating are not the same prospect. Pan-Out estimates the odds a junior athlete reaches each level, across tennis, chess, baseball and hockey.

Five gradient-boosted models each answer one question (trajectory, dominance, clutch, load, and performance against same-age peers). Logistic heads stack them per outcome level, isotonic regression calibrates the odds, and players are split by birth year into train, stack, calibrate and test cohorts. Because the stack is linear, every dossier shows how much each signal moved a player's odds.

On simulated player histories, it ranks future pros far better than rating alone (AUC 0.94 against 0.84 in tennis). Backtested on real ATP careers, the ranking edge nearly vanished: 0.844 against 0.841, though it still found about 2.6 times more future pros among players the rankings rated low. That gap between simulated and real results was the most useful thing the project produced: it measures how much my simulator flattered the model.

Reading a script no one can read

Independent research with Louie Merriam, 2023 to now. Published in the International Journal of Computer Applications, Vol. 187, No. 64. Funded by an Emergent Ventures grant.

The Indus script is around 4,500 years old, has hundreds of signs, and has no bilingual key. It is probably logo-syllabic: some signs stand for whole words, others for sounds. Our paper separates the two using only where signs appear.

    An 18-inscription toy corpus, so the method fits on screen. The paper runs on the full corpus and tests each association statistically.
