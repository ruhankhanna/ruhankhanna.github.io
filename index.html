01athletiq

2023 – now

founder · started freshman year

a recruiting platform college track coaches use to find, compare and keep track of recruits.

9
programs piloted it, incl. stanford, harvard, unc
$30k
raised from angels, microsoft and aws
2
products: athletiq for coaches, pan-out for prospects

i run track, and recruiting looked broken from both sides: coaches scroll through athletic.net by hand, and good runners at small schools never get seen. athletiq scrapes the rankings, puts every race on one scale, forecasts where an athlete is heading, and lets a coach manage a whole recruiting class in one place. coaches at georgetown, haverford, connecticut college and berry use it.

pan-out the second athletiq product

the same engine became pan-out: how good will a 13-year-old become, and which kids is the market underpricing? it prices junior tennis, chess, baseball and hockey prospects from what a ratings export already has. an age-blind rating misses a kid who's outperforming their age, or whose curve is still steepening. every score is a calibrated probability, so a coach or a brand gets odds, not verdicts. it was part of the conversation at an ays sports event in new york on the future of tennis brand deals, which is where i met carlos alcaraz.

02meridian

2025 – now

first author, with prof. adam tashman, uva school of data science

venture capital is a matching market, and it matches badly. i trained a model to rank which investors fit a startup, then measured how far real deals land from the best fit.

60.3%
of real investors were in the model's top 10, out of sample
6
median rank of the investor who actually wrote the check
~48k
investors ranked for every startup

a lightgbm lambdarank model learns from about 62,000 startups and 150,000+ funding events on crunchbase: stage, check size, geography, and how similar a startup is to each investor's past portfolio. the rank of the investor who really invested is the mismatch distance. it's usually small, but the tail is long, and it isn't random. it varies by region, stage and who the founder is. 434, an accelerator in charlottesville, uses it with its founders.

05reachbot

each question pulls the closest passages by vector similarity, and the model may answer only from them. the hard part wasn't retrieval. it was deciding what to do when the evidence is weak. the answer: say less, or say nothing.

00the sim rig

middle school

where it started

i built this in middle school, and it looks like it: a cheap pxn v9 wheel and pedals, a ferrari-style wheel i 3d-printed, and a button box i soldered by hand.

i wanted something closer to the wheel an f1 driver holds, so i designed a face plate and grips, printed them, glued in arcade buttons and wired every one to the box behind it. it's rough: visible glue, wires everywhere. around the same time i wrote a piece on how ferrari could fix its car's aerodynamics. neither was polished, but together they're why, in high school, i went looking for a real car to build and found secret.
