01athletiq

2023 – now

founder · started freshman year

a recruiting platform college track coaches use to find, compare and keep track of recruits.

9
programs piloted it, incl. stanford, harvard, unc
$8k
cloud backing from microsoft and aws
2023
first version, built as a runner

i run track, and recruiting looked broken from both sides: coaches scroll through athletic.net by hand, and good runners at small schools never get seen. athletiq scrapes the rankings, puts every race on one scale, forecasts where an athlete is heading, and lets a coach manage a whole recruiting class in one place. coaches at georgetown, haverford, connecticut college and berry use it.

the same engine became pan-out, which prices junior tennis, chess, baseball and hockey prospects. on simulated data it beat plain ratings easily (auc 0.94 vs 0.84). on real atp careers the edge nearly vanished (0.844 vs 0.841), which taught me more than the first number did.

02meridian

2025 – now

first author, with prof. adam tashman, uva school of data science

venture capital is a matching market, and it matches badly. i trained a model to rank which investors fit a startup, then measured how far real deals land from the best fit.

60.3%
of real investors were in the model's top 10, out of sample
6
median rank of the investor who actually wrote the check
~48k
investors ranked for every startup

a lightgbm lambdarank model learns from about 62,000 startups and 150,000+ funding events on crunchbase: stage, check size, geography, and how similar a startup is to each investor's past portfolio. the rank of the investor who really invested is the mismatch distance. it's usually small, but the tail is long, and it isn't random. it varies by region, stage and who the founder is. 434, an accelerator in charlottesville, uses it with its founders.
