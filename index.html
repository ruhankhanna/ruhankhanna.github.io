athletIQ

Founder and CEO. 2023 to now. Python, Flask, JS.

College track coaches recruit from spreadsheets and a single personal best. A PR tells you the fastest day a kid ever had. It does not tell you where they are now, or where they will be by signing day.

athletIQ rates every recruit on a recency-weighted average with a 90-day half-life, fits a trend through their recent marks, and forecasts 3, 6 and 12 months out. The forecast has a guardrail: if the fit is weak (r² under 0.5), projected improvement is capped at 3% per 90 days, so one lucky race cannot turn a kid into a future champion.

I wrote the rating model, built the product, and sold it. It is under contract with programs including Stanford and UNC, and has been piloted with coaches at Harvard, Georgetown and Texas, with $8K in backing from Microsoft and AWS.

Pan-Out

Solo build. 2026. Python, scikit-learn, a stdlib HTTP server, vanilla JS.

athletIQ taught me that ratings are age-blind. A 13-year-old rated 9.0 and a 17-year-old rated 9.0 are not the same prospect. Pan-Out prices that gap across tennis, chess, baseball and hockey, and states every prediction as a calibrated probability: when it says 30%, about 30% of those kids made it.

Five models, one question each

  1. Trajectory. 12 and 24 month rating slope and acceleration.
  2. Dominance. Game share, win rate, schedule strength.
  3. Clutch. Deciding-set record against their own baseline.
  4. Load. Match volume, trend, volatility.
  5. Age outperformance. Standard deviations above same-age, same-gender peers. The strongest single signal.

A logistic meta-model stacks the five, then isotonic regression calibrates it. Because the stack is linear in the sub-model logits, every player page shows exactly how much each signal moved the odds.

No leakage, by birth year

The model is graded only on cohorts it never touched, the same way it would meet real kids.

Model vs. the rating alone, held-out AUC

The training universes are simulated, because no open bulk API exists for junior ratings. The models only see what a real UTR, USTA or FIDE export would contain. A separate real-data hub runs on about 121,000 FIDE-rated juniors and about 113,000 real pro baseball careers.

Reading a script no one can read

Independent research with Louie Merriam. 2023 to now. Published in the International Journal of Computer Applications, Vol. 187, No. 64.

The Indus script is around 4,500 years old, has hundreds of signs, and has no bilingual key. It is probably logo-syllabic: some signs stand for whole words, others for sounds, and some do both. Our paper asks which is which, using only the statistics of where signs appear.

This is a 14-inscription toy corpus so the method fits on screen. Sign numbers follow the style of the standard concordances. The paper runs on the full corpus and validates associations statistically.

Future Fellows

Founding intern, employee #2. 2026 to now. Python, FastAPI, Claude API.

Future Fellows matches high schoolers with paid, serious work. I am building the system that screens applicants, and the hard part is not the model. It is making sure the model cannot quietly become a résumé filter.

So the constraints live in code, each with a test that fails if someone removes it. Names and schools are scrubbed before scoring. There is no total score and no ranking. Demographics sit in a separate table nobody can read until decisions are made. Every model call is logged with its exact prompt. And no score is accepted unless it comes with a quote that appears word for word in the application.

The live version asks applicants for a public repo, reads the code and its history, then asks questions only the author could answer.
