01athletiq

2023 – now

founder · started it freshman year

software college track coaches use to find recruits, compare them, and keep track of who they're talking to.

products, athletiq for coaches and pan-out for prospects

i run track, so i saw recruiting from the athlete's side first. coaches were scrolling through athletic.net by hand, and fast kids at small schools just never came up. athletiq pulls in the rankings, puts every race on the same scale, estimates where each runner is headed, and gives a coach one place to run a whole recruiting class.

pan-out the second athletiq product

pan-out grew out of the same code. it takes on the question every junior coach and sponsor is quietly asking, which is how good this 13-year-old is going to be. it covers tennis, chess, baseball and hockey, and it gives odds rather than verdicts. it came up at an ays sports event in new york about where tennis brand deals are going, which is also where i got to meet carlos alcaraz.

02meridian

2025 – now

first author, with prof. adam tashman, uva school of data science

i wanted to know how well startups and investors actually find each other. so i built a model that ranks every investor for a given startup, then checked where the real investor landed on that list.

60.3%
of real investors were in the model's top 10, out of sample
6
median rank of the investor who actually wrote the check
~48k
investors ranked for every startup

it learned from about 62,000 startups and 150,000 funding rounds on crunchbase, looking at things like stage, check size, location and what each investor has backed before. most real deals land near the top of the list. the ones that don't aren't random, though. the miss is bigger in some regions and stages, and for some founders more than others. 434, an accelerator in charlottesville, now uses it with its founders.

04secret

2023 – now

brought the team back · build lead

sidwell's electric car team hadn't raced since 2016. i found it still listed on the school website, talked a few friends and two teachers into it, and we started racing again.

2016
the last time sidwell had raced, before us
1st
"best use of 3d printing," a first for the school

the dc electric vehicle grand prix comes down to one thing, which is how far you can go in an hour on one battery. we rebuilt our kit car with an aluminum chassis, a new battery pack, 3d-printed wheel covers and an arduino that helps the driver pace. we raised the money ourselves and learned a lot from umd's formula team. you can spin the car below, pull it apart, and click on any part.

05reachbot

summer 2026

summer associate, reach capital

reach's founders kept asking questions the firm had already answered somewhere, in a talk or a memo. reachbot finds that answer and shows exactly where it came from.

150–200
founders and portfolio companies using it
696
talks, memos and recordings indexed
$0.005
cost per answer

it looks up the passages closest to your question and only answers from those. getting it to find things turned out to be the easy part. the harder part was teaching it to say less, or nothing at all, when the library doesn't really cover what you asked.

07stackup

2026 – now

built it · employee #2 at future fellows

a free, ten-minute look at code a student actually wrote. it reads the repo and its history, judges how hard and how good the work is, and then asks a few questions only the author could answer.

10 min
start to finish, free
5
questions written for your repo
0
total scores. difficulty and quality stay separate

future fellows finds young people paid project work with real employers. i built stackup so that what a student has made counts for more than a polished essay or an expensive summer program. the ai gathers the evidence, a person makes the call, and every score has to quote the actual code. here's a run on two sample repos.

00the sim rig

middle school

where it started

i built this in middle school and it shows. a cheap pxn v9 wheel and pedals, a ferrari-style wheel i 3d-printed, and a button box i soldered myself.

i wanted a wheel like the ones f1 drivers hold, so i designed the face plate and grips, printed them, glued in arcade buttons and wired each one to the box. there's glue everywhere and the wiring is a mess. around then i also wrote a medium post about how ferrari could fix its aero. neither was exactly good, but they're the reason i went looking for a real car to work on in high school and found secret.
